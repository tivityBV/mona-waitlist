Make offline normal again

About Mona Offline

Mona Offline is an Android app that puts your phone offline by default. Offline is where your phone starts, rather than a short break or a punishment. If you want to go online, you dial in. And just like in the old days, that takes a moment.

The ritual

You tap "Dial in". A real modem handshake plays, about sixteen seconds of screeching and hiss, exactly the way a dial-up modem sounded in the nineties. Then CONNECT 14400 appears, with the question of how long you want to be online: three, seven or thirty minutes. You choose, the timer runs, and when the time is up the line drops with NO CARRIER. Your phone is offline again. After that there is a short wait before you can dial in once more.

Those sixteen seconds are not loading time. There is nothing to speed up, because they are the product. They give you room to ask yourself what you actually came to do.

Why friction is the point

Most of the moments when we reach for our phone happen without a decision. It is a reflex: a dead moment, a hand already moving, and ten minutes later you no longer know why you started. Apps are built so that the reflex pays off.

You can put a lock against that, but hard blockers work until they hurt, and then they disappear from the phone. Mona takes another road. The threshold is low enough to get over whenever you really want something, and high enough to interrupt the reflex. Nothing is forbidden to you. You are simply asked whether you mean it.

That is also why Mona is never strict or preachy. There are no points, no badges, no streaks you can break and no notifications that call you back. The app only shows how often you dialed in. People get used to warnings, but not to seeing themselves.

What stays reachable

Offline is wonderful until your bank, your navigation or your messages are gone at the wrong moment. That is why Mona has a list of apps that stay reachable, even when the line is closed. You put that list together yourself: your messaging app, your bank, your transit app, your maps. Whatever is on your list notices nothing of the block.

The block itself works through a local VPN connection. Local means literally local here: there is no server, your traffic passes nowhere, and nothing is looked at or kept. The connection exists only to be able to put your phone offline.

What Mona is not

No productivity app with charts you have to beat. No digital detox in ten steps. No subscription you cancel and then forget about.

And no unbreakable lock. Anyone who wants to switch the app off can do so. That is a deliberate choice: software that holds you hostage gets thrown away. Mona wants to be the app you keep, and that only works if having it around is not annoying.

Where it comes from

Mona Offline is made by tivity B.V. in Amsterdam. The app is in a closed test with a small group of users who use it every day and say honestly what is wrong with it. What they report decides what gets built.

The app is free and free of advertising, and it holds no ad network and no tracker. Whatever data does go around, and that is remarkably little, is on the privacy page.
